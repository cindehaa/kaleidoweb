The
Manhattan

Everything this drink is lives in one ratio — two of whiskey to one of sweet vermouth, tilted bitter by two dashes. Hold the ratio and you can carry the recipe in your head forever; move any part of it and the drink changes its name.

I — Stir

All three into a mixing glass over ice, stirred until well-chilled. Stirring, not shaking: the ice gives its cold and a little of its water, and the drink stays silk-clear.

II — Strain

Strained up into a chilled Nick & Nora or coupe. Nothing else goes in the glass — no ice to keep diluting what the stir already finished.

III — Garnish

A brandied cherry, dropped in — or a lemon twist if you'd rather end on brightness than depth.

Move one ingredient and it becomes a different drink.

The Manhattan sits at the center of a small family. Each neighbor is one substitution away — the ratio barely moves. (The family tree is bar tradition, not part of the source recipe.)

The original measure. Rye for spice or bourbon for roundness; sweet vermouth carries the red fruit; the cherry agrees with it.

Where it began

New York, sometime in the 1870s–80s — beyond that, the record argues with itself. The famous story puts the first Manhattan at the Manhattan Club, at a banquet involving Winston Churchill's mother; historians consider that legend, not fact, since she was in England at the time. What's solid is simpler: the drink is among the oldest cocktails to marry American whiskey with Italian vermouth, and it never left the repertoire. — our summary of the drink's commonly told history; the origin is contested.

The source recipe, as published
  • 2 ounces bourbon or rye
  • 1 ounce sweet vermouth
  • 2 dashes Angostura bitters
  • Garnish: brandied cherry

Stir with ice until well-chilled → strain into a chilled Nick & Nora or coupe → garnish with a brandied cherry, or a lemon twist.
